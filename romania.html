ROMANIA · BANGLADESH STUDENT GUIDE

Study in Romania
from Bangladesh.

Romania offers comparatively low European living costs, English, French and Romanian-taught degrees, a large public-university network, and a government scholarship open to non-EU applicants. For Bangladeshi students, the route is structured around the Ministry of Education Letter of Acceptance, the D/SD long-stay study visa, and a residence permit after arrival.

Romania is fully in Schengen, but the student visa is still a national long-stay visa

Romania became a full Schengen member on 1 January 2025. Non-EU students who need a visa still use the Romanian long-stay visa for studies, marked D/SD, then obtain a Romanian residence permit after arrival.

ADMISSION

Romania uses a national Letter of Acceptance for non-EU students, but universities still control programme selection.

Non-EU applicants normally submit their academic file to the chosen institution, which sends the approved application onward for the Ministry of Education Letter of Acceptance where required.

Bachelor

Completed upper secondary education

Bangladeshi students normally apply with SSC and HSC documents, properly legalised or authenticated as required.

Master

Recognised Bachelor degree

Programme relevance, entrance exam, GPA and language requirements vary by university.

PhD

Master degree + doctoral selection

Research proposal, supervisor fit, interview and faculty-specific requirements are common.

The official application portal allows students to apply to up to five programmes in the same study cycle. Always confirm whether your university uses the central portal or its own international-admissions workflow.

INTAKES

October is the normal academic start, but applications begin much earlier.

Most universities run admission sessions between February and early September. Non-EU students should apply earlier because the Letter of Acceptance and visa process add extra steps.

MAIN INTAKE

September / October

The broadest programme choice for Bachelor, Master and PhD study.

OTHER STARTS

Programme-specific

Some universities may offer additional sessions, but Romania should generally be planned around the autumn academic year.

The official application FAQ says most admission sessions run between February and the beginning of September. Visa applicants should not wait until the final university deadline.

You can choose a foreign-language programme and meet that university’s language requirement, or study in Romanian after completing the required proficiency route.

LIVING COST

Romania remains one of the more affordable study destinations in the EU.

The official Study in Romania portal uses approximately €600 per month as a general living-cost estimate, while housing can vary sharply by city and accommodation type.

TUITION

Romanian tuition can be comparatively low, but the range is wide.

The official Study in Romania portal lists international tuition from roughly €400 per year up to almost €9,000 depending on field and institution, while some specialised or medical programmes can be higher.

Lower-cost programmes

From ~€400 / year

Some programmes at public universities can sit at the low end of the national range.

Typical non-EU planning

Often several thousand euro / year

Engineering, business, computing and sciences commonly sit in the middle of the range.

Medicine / specialised

Higher-cost category

Medical, dental and selected professional programmes can be significantly more expensive than general degrees.

Always use the exact programme page. The national portal itself warns that tuition depends on study field and country category.

ROMANIAN GOVERNMENT SCHOLARSHIP

Bangladeshi applicants are eligible in principle under the non-EU scholarship scheme.

The annual Ministry of Foreign Affairs scholarship is open to citizens of non-EU countries, subject to the programme exclusions and eligibility rules.

D/SD STUDY VISA · BANGLADESH

The student route has two immigration stages: visa first, residence permit after arrival.

Non-EU students who require a visa apply for the Romanian long-stay visa for studies, marked D/SD. The visa allows entry; the residence permit is what supports longer-term legal stay during the degree.

01

Apply to the university

Submit the academic and language file to the selected institution.

02

Obtain Letter of Acceptance

Non-EU study requires the Ministry of Education acceptance process where applicable.

03

Pay tuition where required

Non-scholarship applicants normally need proof of tuition payment for the visa file.

04

Prepare D/SD documents

Passport, acceptance, funds, criminal record, insurance, accommodation and language evidence where required.

05

Submit Romanian long-stay visa

Use the official eVisa and Romanian diplomatic mission process serving Bangladesh.

06

Travel to Romania

The long-stay visa is valid for entry and a limited initial stay.

07

Apply for residence permit

Submit to IGI at least 30 days before the visa-based stay expires.

08

Renew during study

Keep enrolment, accommodation, insurance and financial evidence current.

The D/SD visa supporting documents currently include medical insurance with at least €30,000 coverage for the intended visa period. Always verify the current eVisa checklist before submission.

SPOUSE · CHILDREN

Family reunification is a separate immigration decision.

A student residence permit does not automatically place family members on the same status. Eligible relatives use Romania’s family reunification process and must meet the applicable accommodation, insurance and financial requirements.

Planning rule

Assess family before accepting the offer

If spouse or children will accompany you, confirm the current IGI family-reunification route and required income before paying non-refundable fees.

Work

Dependant work rights are separate

Do not assume a family residence automatically creates the same student work rights as the principal applicant.

General Inspectorate for Immigration ↗
WORK WHILE STUDYING

Current guidance allows non-EU students to work up to 6 hours per day without a separate work permit.

As long as you hold a Romanian residence permit for study, you can take part-time employment under the current student rule without obtaining a separate work permit.

Current limit

Up to 6 hours / day

The current Study in Romania portal and 2026 IGI guidance use a six-hour daily student employment limit.

Permit

No separate work permit within the limit

The student residence permit is sufficient for eligible part-time employment under the current rule.

More than the limit

Different authorization applies

If you want to work beyond the student allowance, confirm the appropriate employment authorization with IGI.

Some older official pages still show a previous four-hour limit. This guide follows the newer 2026 IGI student notice and current Study in Romania portal, both of which state six hours per day.

HEALTH INSURANCE

Insurance is required for both visa and residence stages.

The D/SD visa requires travel medical insurance, and the residence permit requires valid health coverage in Romania.

Visa

At least €30,000 coverage

The current eVisa checklist requires insurance covering emergency treatment, hospitalisation, repatriation and related risks.

Residence

Maintain Romanian coverage

Study in Romania notes different health-insurance arrangements depending on age and status.

AFTER GRADUATION

Romania offers up to 9 months to search for work or prepare a business.

IGI allows the temporary study residence to be extended after graduation for job search, employment formalities or opening a business.

Job search / business

Up to 9 months

Graduates can extend their right of stay for up to nine months after graduation with proof of completed studies.

Academic completion

Separate 6-month option

IGI also provides a shorter extension for completing academic formalities and authenticating study documents where needed.

The nine-month period is a transition window, not an automatic permanent work status. A graduate who secures employment must complete the appropriate employment and residence formalities.

WHERE TO STUDY

Romania has several genuine university cities.

Choose based on programme quality, housing, internships and language environment rather than assuming Bucharest is the only serious option.

Bucharest

Largest university + employer market

Broadest institutional choice and professional opportunities, with higher housing costs.

Cluj-Napoca

Technology + student city

Strong universities, IT sector and large international student community.

Iași

Historic academic centre

Major public universities and comparatively manageable living costs.

Timișoara

Engineering + western Romania

Technology, manufacturing and strong university environment near Central Europe.

Brașov

Smaller-city balance

University study with lower-scale city life and access to central Romania.

Constanța

Coastal option

Selected medical, engineering and maritime study opportunities.

AFTER ARRIVAL

The D/SD visa is only the entry stage.

International students need to complete the Romanian residence-permit process after enrolment and before the initial visa-based stay expires.

01

Complete university enrolment

Submit originals and obtain the active student certificate.

02

Confirm accommodation

Keep valid proof of where you live in Romania.

03

Arrange health coverage

Make sure your insurance meets residence requirements.

04

Apply to IGI

Submit the residence-permit application at least 30 days before the visa expires.

05

Renew as required

Keep enrolment and immigration status valid throughout the degree.
