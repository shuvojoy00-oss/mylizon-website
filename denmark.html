Major change from 1 October 2026

For many third-country students applying for a residence permit on or after 1 October 2026 in state-approved professional Bachelor, Bachelor, Master and related programmes, the post-study job-seeking period is generally reduced from up to 3 years to up to 1 year. Accompanying family can generally no longer receive a student-family residence permit under these rules. PhD students are not affected in the same way.

BANGLADESH ELIGIBILITY

There is no one Denmark-wide HSC or CGPA answer.

Foreign qualifications must be comparable to the relevant Danish qualification, and individual institutions assess specific subjects, language and programme requirements.

HSC

Bachelor eligibility is institution-specific.

For undergraduate admission, the qualification must normally be comparable to a Danish upper-secondary leaving certificate. Some foreign qualifications can require supplementary study.

Quota 2

International school qualifications often enter through quota 2.

At institutions such as the University of Copenhagen, non-EU/EEA applicants with foreign upper-secondary credentials apply through quota 2 and can face tests/individual assessment.

Language

Many Danish Bachelor programmes are taught in Danish.

English-taught Bachelor and professional programmes exist, but programme choice is narrower than at Master’s level.

Masters

A Bachelor comparable to 180 ECTS is the starting point.

Aarhus University, for example, requires a completed Bachelor or equivalent comparable to a Danish Bachelor in level and duration, plus programme-specific prerequisites.

Specific subjects

Subject matching matters.

Science, engineering, economics and other programmes can require specific prior coursework. A general CGPA alone cannot predict admission.

Document verification

Bangladesh degrees can receive additional verification.

The Danish Agency for Higher Education and Science states that Bangladesh higher-education transcripts may need direct institutional verification where online verification is unavailable.

DENMARK'S HIGHER EDUCATION SYSTEM

University is only one part of Danish higher education.

Universities

Research-based Bachelor, Master and PhD study, including Copenhagen, Aarhus, DTU, Aalborg, SDU and CBS.

University Colleges

Professional Bachelor programmes with strong practical and employment links.

Business Academies

Academy Profession degrees and selected top-up routes with applied learning.

State approval matters

Immigration rights differ sharply between state-approved and non-state-approved programmes. Always check the exact programme before paying tuition.

IELTS · TOEFL · ENGLISH

English B is the usual national reference point for English-taught study.

Typical benchmark

Study in Denmark says English-taught programmes commonly require English comparable to Danish English B, roughly around IELTS 6.5 or TOEFL iBT 79–93, while English A is often around IELTS 7.0.

Institution decides

These are planning benchmarks, not one universal score. The individual university or programme sets the accepted test and exact minimum.

MONEY & SELF-SUPPORT

Denmark’s 2026 self-support benchmark is DKK 7,426 per month where proof is required.

2026 SIRI benchmark

Per monthDKK 7,426
Maximum proof period12 months
12-month equivalentDKK 89,112
Family under old/eligible rules+ DKK 7,426 each / month

Full-degree tuition can change the proof requirement.

For a full higher-education degree, SIRI states that if you have paid the tuition fee yourself, that payment can satisfy the self-support requirement. If you do not pay tuition, for example because of a tuition waiver, you may need to document scholarship/living funds at the monthly benchmark.

LIVING COST

Copenhagen is expensive. Smaller Danish cities can change the budget.

Rent

Study in Denmark currently shows a broad monthly example of roughly DKK 3,000–6,500, with city and accommodation type making a major difference.

Food

Official budgeting examples use roughly DKK 2,000–3,500 per month.

Transport

Study in Denmark uses around DKK 300/month as a basic example, though actual commuting can be higher.

Other expenses

Books, phone, insurance and personal costs should be budgeted separately rather than trying to live exactly on the SIRI minimum.

SCHOLARSHIPS

Danish government scholarships are administered by universities.

Danish Government Scholarships

Danish universities receive a limited number of scholarships for highly qualified non-EU/EEA full-degree students.

Tuition waiver + living grant

A scholarship can consist of a full or partial tuition waiver and/or a grant toward living costs. The institution decides the award structure.

PhD funding

Many Danish PhD positions are salaried employment rather than ordinary self-funded student places.

SIRI STUDENT RESIDENCE PERMIT

Denmark uses ST1 for the ordinary higher-education student permit process.

01

Choose a state-approved programme

Check programme approval before paying because work, family and post-study rights can differ for non-state-approved study.

02

Receive admission

You must be admitted to the Danish higher-education programme before applying.

03

Tuition & finances

Pay required tuition and prepare self-support evidence where applicable.

04

Create SIRI case order ID

Create the case order ID and pay the study-case fee before submitting ST1.

05

Complete ST1

Your educational institution and you each complete part of the application. Online ST1 is available.

06

Submit & record biometrics

Submit online or through the relevant diplomatic/application-centre process and record biometrics within the required timeframe.

07

SIRI decision

SIRI assesses programme, finances, study purpose and the other residence conditions.

08

Travel & register

After arrival, register your Danish address/CPR when eligible and complete the practical setup for healthcare and tax.

PERMIT COSTS

SIRI increased case-processing fees in 2026.

Study case fee

DKK 3,060 for 2026 SIRI study applications, subject to applicable exemptions.

Accompanying family fee

DKK 3,080 for eligible accompanying-family applications in 2026.

Other costs

Biometrics/application-centre, document, travel and local registration costs can be separate.

DOCUMENTS · BANGLADESH

Admission documents and SIRI residence documents serve different purposes.

Academic admission

SSC/HSC or prior degree certificates and transcripts, specific subject documentation, English evidence, CV/SOP/references where the institution requires them, and programme-specific prerequisite evidence.

Master’s prerequisites

Degree certificate, complete transcript, course descriptions or ECTS-equivalent subject evidence can be needed to prove that the Bachelor matches the Danish Master’s prerequisites.

Bangladesh transcript verification

For formal Danish qualification assessment, the Danish Agency may require the Bangladesh institution to send an academic transcript directly where official online verification is unavailable.

SIRI permit documents

Passport, admission, ST1 information, tuition evidence, self-support evidence where required, and any documents requested through the case process.

Employment history

ST1 requires information about previous education and work experience. Keep the timeline consistent with your admission documents and CV.

SPOUSE & FAMILY · NEW 2026 RULE

For most new ordinary students, accompanying-family rights have been removed.

Applications from 1 October 2026

If the student applies on or after 1 October 2026 for the affected state-approved Bachelor/Master-type study routes, spouse/partner and children generally cannot obtain accompanying-family residence permits under the student rules.

Applications before 1 October 2026

Transitional rules can preserve family eligibility where the relevant student/family applications were submitted before the cut-off.

PhD students

PhD students remain outside this restriction and can still have qualifying spouse/partner and children under 18 apply as accompanying family.

WORK WHILE STUDYING

Denmark uses 90 hours per month — not a simple weekly cap.

September to May

Eligible non-EU/EEA students in state-approved higher education can work up to 90 hours per month.

June, July, August

Eligible students can work full-time during the summer months.

Your residence card controls

Always check the exact work permission printed on your residence card because non-state-approved study and special cases can differ.

Official student-work guidance ↗
CPR & HEALTHCARE

After CPR registration, non-EU students gain access to Denmark’s public healthcare system.

CPR registration

Non-EU students staying more than three months need the relevant residence permit and then register in Denmark’s Civil Registration System where eligible.

Yellow health card

After registration, the national health-insurance card is normally sent to the Danish address and shows your CPR number and assigned GP.

WORK PERMITS AFTER STUDY

Graduate job seeking is the bridge, not permanent residence.

Positive List · Higher Education

From 1 July 2026 the Positive List for People with a Higher Education contains 164 shortage job titles.

Pay Limit Scheme

For 2026, the ordinary Pay Limit Scheme annual minimum is DKK 552,000.

Supplementary Pay Limit

The 2026 supplementary pay-limit threshold is DKK 446,000 per year.

Other work schemes

Fast-track, researcher, Positive List and other SIRI work schemes can be relevant depending on employer, occupation and salary.

CITIES

Denmark is compact, but cost and study culture still vary by city.

Copenhagen

Largest university/employer market with UCPH, CBS, DTU nearby and the highest housing pressure.

Aarhus

Major student city built around Aarhus University with a strong technology, business and research ecosystem.

Aalborg

Known for problem-based learning, engineering and a lower-scale urban environment.

Odense

University city with SDU and strong robotics, technology and healthcare clusters.

ARRIVAL

The Danish system becomes much easier once your CPR is active.

Before departure

Passport, residence permit/decision, admission, tuition evidence, accommodation, finances and institution arrival instructions.

Arrival

Move into the registered address and follow your municipality/international-house instructions for civil registration.

CPR & healthcare

Register in the CPR system where eligible, choose/receive GP details and wait for the yellow health card.

Working

Check your residence card before employment, keep within the 90-hour monthly limit during September–May and understand Danish tax registration.
