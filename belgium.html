BANGLADESH ELIGIBILITY

Belgium does not have one national HSC or CGPA cut-off.

Admission is institution-specific, and the rules also differ between Flanders and the French-speaking system. Foreign qualifications are normally assessed individually.

HSC

Bachelor entry depends on the institution.

In Flanders, a foreign secondary diploma normally needs to give access to university studies in the country where it was awarded, often supported by an access declaration.

Equivalence

French-speaking Bachelor study can involve diploma-equivalence rules.

Admission committees and legal equivalence requirements can differ by program and institution. Check the exact French-speaking institution before applying.

Masters

Your previous degree is assessed academically.

Institutions consider degree recognition, subject background, grades, prerequisites and language. There is no Belgium-wide CGPA conversion.

Low CGPA

Course fit and institution choice matter.

A lower result does not create one automatic national outcome. Preparatory study, different institutions or a better-matched program can change the route.

Study Gap

Explain why study now makes sense.

Employment, business, research and other activity can support a coherent academic and career progression.

PhD

Doctoral students can fall under student rules.

A PhD candidate registered as a student at a university is treated as a student for the student-visa route, unlike many postdoctoral researchers.

FLANDERS VS FRENCH-SPEAKING BELGIUM

Choose the education system before comparing universities.

Flanders + Brussels

Dutch-speaking higher education system

Includes KU Leuven, Ghent University, University of Antwerp, Vrije Universiteit Brussel, Hasselt University and universities of applied sciences and arts. Many Master’s programs are taught in English.

Wallonia-Brussels

French-speaking higher education system

Includes UCLouvain, Université libre de Bruxelles, University of Liège, University of Mons, University of Namur plus university colleges and schools of arts. Many Master’s programs also have English options.

LANGUAGE

Belgium is multilingual, and program language matters more than the country name.

English-taught programs

Many Master’s programs are offered in English, especially in Flanders and Brussels. Each institution decides accepted English tests and minimum scores.

Dutch

Dutch is important for many Bachelor programs in Flanders and can improve daily life and employment opportunities.

French

French remains central in Wallonia-Brussels. Even when a Master’s is taught in English, French can matter significantly for student life and work.

INTAKES & DEADLINES

September is the main start — but non-EEA deadlines can be much earlier.

Belgian institutions often use one major academic-year intake. Scholarship and non-EEA admission deadlines can fall between January and spring, so planning should start early.

LIVING COST

Brussels is not Liège, Ghent or a smaller university town.

Housing

Student rooms, university residences, private shared housing and studios are common. French-speaking guidance currently shows around €400–€600/month as one planning range.

Real monthly budget

French-speaking official guidance uses roughly €1,100–€1,300/month for a realistic overall student budget, above the immigration minimum.

Transport

Belgium is compact, with strong rail and public transport links. Student transport discounts can vary by region and operator.

SCHOLARSHIPS

Flanders and Wallonia-Brussels have different flagship funding routes.

Master Mind · Flanders

The 2026–27 Master Mind Scholarship is €10,225 plus a tuition-fee waiver structure for selected Master’s students. The 2026–27 call is now closed.

WBI Master IN

Wallonia-Brussels International’s 2026–27 Master grant provides €10,020 paid over 12 months. It does not cover the full cost of the year.

ARES

ARES offers selected advanced Bachelor, Master and continuing-education scholarships to eligible applicants from partner countries/programs.

03

Prepare solvency

Use a blocked account, bank guarantee, scholarship or valid Annex 32 sponsorship route.

04

Medical & required documents

Use a Belgian-authorized doctor where the Bangladesh checklist requires the medical certificate and prepare the full Visa D file.

05

VisaOnWeb

Complete the long-stay visa application electronically and prepare the printed/signed forms.

06

Book VFS Dhaka

Belgian visa services are now available in Bangladesh through the Belgian Visa Application Centre in Banani, Dhaka.

07

Submit Visa D

Submit the documents, biometrics and applicable fees. From 1 July 2026, the long-stay visa handling fee is €250. A separate Immigration Office contribution may also apply unless exempt.

08

Decision & travel

Travel after approval and follow your university and municipality registration instructions after arrival.

VISA COSTS

Belgian student applications can involve more than one government charge.

Visa D handling fee

€250 from 1 July 2026 for long-stay visa handling.

Immigration Office contribution

Some Visa D applicants must pay a separate administrative contribution directly to the Immigration Office before submission. Exemptions exist.

VFS / supporting costs

Application-centre service charges, medical certificate, legalization/translation and other document costs are separate.

DOCUMENTS · BANGLADESH

Belgium’s student file is very document-driven.

Admission & academic documents

Passport, admission/pre-registration/registration attestation, diploma and academic records giving access to Belgian higher education, language evidence and any institution-specific documents.

Financial evidence

Blocked-account confirmation, qualifying bank guarantee, scholarship evidence or Annex 32 formal obligation, depending on the route used.

Annex 32 sponsor documents

Relationship evidence, sponsor identity/status, income evidence, employment certificate and recent payslips or business/tax evidence can be required depending on the sponsor. The formal obligation must follow Belgian authentication rules.

Medical certificate

Use the doctors certified by the Belgian authorities for Bangladesh when the student checklist requires a medical certificate.

Police certificate

Follow the current Bangladesh student Visa D checklist. Do not assume the documentation of another Belgian mission is identical to Dhaka.

Translation / legalization

Documents can require translation or legalization depending on language and document type. Follow the Bangladesh checklist and institution instructions.

SPOUSE & FAMILY

Family reunion is a separate Visa D process.

Spouse / partner / minor children

Belgium provides family-reunification routes for eligible family members of non-EU residents, subject to the conditions of the sponsor’s residence status.

Separate application

Family members do not simply “attach” to the student visa. They use the relevant family-reunification Visa D route and document checklist.

Housing, insurance & means

Family-reunification applications can require proof of suitable housing, health insurance and sufficient/stable resources under the applicable rules.

WORK WHILE STUDYING

Non-EU students with limited labour-market access can work up to 20 hours per week during the academic year.

20 hours / week

During the school year, work must remain compatible with your studies and is limited to 20 hours per week for students with labour-market-limited residence status.

School holidays

Eligible students can work without that weekly hour limit during official school holidays.

Student work rules

Belgium also has specific student-employment social-security rules. Check Student At Work for your current student-hours account and employment conditions.

HEALTH INSURANCE

Health insurance is mandatory for international students.

Belgian health insurance

Non-EEA students should take out Belgian health insurance. With proof of enrolment, students can join a Belgian health-insurance provider.

International cover

Students may also need proof of international medical coverage for part of the immigration/arrival process, depending on their situation.

AFTER STUDY

Belgium offers a 12-month orientation year.

Orientation year

Length12 months
PurposeFind work or start a business
Residence cardA card
Extendable?No

You can work during the orientation year.

The orientation year gives access to the Belgian labour market while you search for a qualifying longer-term work or self-employment residence route. The 12-month period includes the time covered by Annex 15 while the application is pending.

WORK & LONG-TERM STAY

The orientation year is a bridge, not permanent residence.

Single Permit

A Belgian employer can initiate a single-permit route combining work and residence authorization where the job and regional rules qualify.

EU Blue Card

Highly qualified employment can also use the EU Blue Card framework where salary, qualification and employment conditions are met.

Self-employment

Students using the orientation year to start a business need to move into the relevant professional-card/residence framework.

Long-term residence

Permanent/long-term residence is a separate legal pathway based on qualifying lawful residence and other conditions. A Belgian degree alone does not guarantee it.

CITIES

Belgium is compact, but the student experience changes by city and language region.

Brussels

International capital with both Dutch- and French-speaking universities, EU institutions and higher housing costs.

Leuven

Major student city built around KU Leuven, with a dense academic environment.

Ghent

Large Flemish student city with Ghent University and applied-science institutions.

Antwerp

Large port and business city with university, management and applied-study options.

Louvain-la-Neuve

Purpose-built French-speaking university city centred around UCLouvain.

Liège / Mons / Namur

French-speaking university cities with different housing costs and program strengths.

ARRIVAL

After Visa D approval, Belgium still has local registration steps.

Before departure

Passport, Visa D, admission/enrolment documents, solvency proof, accommodation, insurance, university arrival instructions and key legalized documents.

Arrival

Travel to your accommodation, contact the institution and follow the local commune/municipality residence-registration procedure for long-stay residents.

First week

University registration, residence-card process, health insurance, bank account, phone, transport and student services.

First month

Understand employment limits, tenancy, academic-progress requirements and the language environment of your city.
