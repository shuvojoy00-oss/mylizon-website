SINGAPORE · BANGLADESH STUDENT GUIDE

Study in Singapore
from Bangladesh.

Singapore combines globally recognised universities, a highly connected Asian business environment, English-medium education and strong graduate career opportunities. For Bangladeshi students, the decision must also account for high living costs, competitive admission, Student's Pass rules and the fact that post-study employment requires a separate work pass.

Singapore in one view

Strong fit for academically competitive students who want English-medium study, Asian career exposure and internationally recognised universities.

A Singapore Student's Pass is not an open work visa and graduation does not automatically create one

Eligible students at approved institutions can work under specific exemptions while studying. After graduation, eligible Institute of Higher Learning graduates may apply for an ICA Long-Term Visit Pass to seek employment, but actual employment still requires the appropriate MOM work pass.

ADMISSION

Singapore admission is highly institution and programme specific.

Autonomous universities assess academic results, subject prerequisites, English evidence, standardised tests where relevant, portfolios, interviews and competition within the applicant pool.

Bachelor

Strong HSC or equivalent profile

Bangladeshi applicants should expect competitive academic screening, especially at NUS, NTU and SMU.

Master

Relevant Bachelor degree

Programme fit, GPA, work experience, GMAT or GRE and English requirements vary by course.

PhD

Research record + faculty fit

Doctoral admission commonly depends on academic strength, research proposal, supervisor fit and funding availability.

Singapore does not use one national admission cut-off for Bangladeshi applicants. Each university publishes its own accepted qualifications and programme criteria.
HIGHER EDUCATION SYSTEM

Singapore has a small but highly selective university system.

The main autonomous universities sit alongside polytechnics, specialist institutions and approved offshore campuses.

Autonomous universitiesResearch + professionalNUS, NTU, SMU, SIT, SUSS and SUTD.
PolytechnicsApplied diploma educationFive major public polytechnics with industry-linked programmes.

Global campusesInternational graduate studyINSEAD, ESSEC, TUM Asia and others recognised by ICA.
Private educationCheck EduTrustOnly EduTrust-certified private institutions can normally enrol foreign students on a Student's Pass.
UNIVERSITIES

The major choice is not city. It is institution, programme and career fit.

Singapore is compact, so programme strength and tuition matter more than regional location.

INTAKES

August is the main university intake.

Most undergraduate programmes start in August. January entry exists for selected postgraduate and institution-specific programmes.

MAIN INTAKE

August

Broadest undergraduate and postgraduate programme choice.

SECONDARY

January, selected programmes

More common in postgraduate study and selected private or specialist institutions.

For Institutes of Higher Learning, ICA says Student's Pass applications should generally be submitted at least two months and not more than three months before the course begins.
ENGLISH

English is the main academic language across Singapore higher education.

Exact English proof depends on qualification type and university. Some applicants can satisfy the requirement through prior English-medium education, while others may need IELTS, TOEFL or another accepted test.

LIVING COST

Singapore is academically attractive but financially demanding.

NUS currently estimates roughly S$1,200 to S$2,100 per month for students living on campus and S$1,550 to S$3,400 off campus, depending heavily on housing and lifestyle.

NUS monthly planning examples

On-campus accommodation~S$500–900
Off-campus accommodation~S$650–1,800
Food~S$300–800
Public transport~S$100–300
Total monthly essentials~S$1,200–3,400
Postgraduate students often face higher housing pressure because university accommodation is limited and priority may be given to other groups.

TUITION + MOE TUITION GRANT

International tuition depends heavily on whether the student receives the MOE Tuition Grant.

Autonomous universities publish subsidised international fees and higher non-subsidised fees. International students taking the Tuition Grant are subject to its service-obligation conditions.

SMU 2026 example

S$26,200 / year subsidised

Many non-ASEAN undergraduate programmes at SMU list this 2026 to 27 fee for international students with the Tuition Grant.

SMU unsubsidised example

S$47,700 / year

The same broad programme group is materially more expensive without the subsidy.

NTU 2026 example

~S$21,400 subsidised

Many programmes list this non-ASEAN international fee, while non-subsidised fees can exceed S$36,000 to S$40,000 depending on programme type.

Bangladesh is not an ASEAN member, so Bangladeshi students normally fall under the non-ASEAN international fee category unless another specific classification applies.

SCHOLARSHIPS

Bangladeshi applicants can compete for major university scholarships, but not every regional scholarship includes Bangladesh.

For example, NUS offers an International Undergraduate Scholarship to exceptional students from all countries except Singapore, while the ASEAN Undergraduate Scholarship is limited to ASEAN citizens and therefore does not include Bangladesh.

NUS International Undergraduate Scholarship

Bangladesh eligible

The scholarship is open to citizens of all countries except Singapore and covers 100% of subsidised tuition after the MOE Tuition Grant, plus living and accommodation allowances.

ASEAN scholarships

Bangladesh not eligible by nationality

Bangladesh is not an ASEAN member, so applicants should not be marketed ASEAN-only awards.

Some international scholarships require recipients to take the MOE Tuition Grant. The grant can carry a post-graduation service obligation, so scholarship value and bond conditions must be reviewed together.

STUDENT'S PASS · BANGLADESH

University admission comes first. ICA Student's Pass approval comes next.

Foreigners accepted as full-time students at an approved Institute of Higher Learning generally need an ICA Student's Pass unless they already hold another qualifying immigration status.

01

Secure full-time admission

Receive the Registration Acknowledgement Letter or equivalent school reference required for ICA.

02

Prepare ICA application

Passport details, education history, financial-support information, family particulars, Singapore address and a recent photograph are commonly required.

03

Apply online

ICA says IHL students should generally apply two to three months before the course begins.

04

Pay S$45 processing fee

The Student's Pass application processing fee is non-refundable.

05

Wait for IPA

ICA states typical processing is within one week, or within two weeks if an entry visa is required, though some cases take longer.

06

Travel after approval

Bangladeshi ordinary passport holders require an entry visa, and ICA approval instructions should be followed before travel.

07

Complete biometrics / formalities

ICA or the university may arrange Student's Pass enrolment after arrival.

08

Pay issuance fee

Current issuance is S$60, plus S$30 for a Multiple Journey Visa if applicable.

The Singapore High Commission in Dhaka also confirms that ordinary Bangladeshi passport holders require an entry visa. That entry visa is separate from the Student's Pass itself.
ICA Student's Pass for IHLs ↗
SPOUSE · CHILDREN

An adult university Student's Pass does not create a general dependant route.

Family planning in Singapore is more restrictive than in many study destinations. A spouse or child of an adult university student normally needs an independent qualifying immigration basis.

Adult university student

No general spouse dependant pass through Student's Pass

Do not assume family can automatically accompany the principal student under the same immigration status.

Child student exception

One parent or grandparent may qualify

ICA has a specific LTVP category allowing one parent or grandparent of a child studying on a Student's Pass, subject to local sponsorship and eligibility.

ICA parent / grandparent LTVP ↗
WORK WHILE STUDYING

Eligible full-time students can work up to 16 hours per week during term.

The exemption only applies to full-time students holding a Student's Pass and studying at institutions on MOM's approved list.

During term

Maximum 16 hours / week

No separate work pass is required when all MOM eligibility conditions are met.

Internship

Can exceed ordinary hour rule when part of course

An industrial attachment or internship can qualify if it is conducted by the institution and contributes to graduation requirements.

Important

Not every school qualifies

Private or exchange students should verify whether their institution and programme fall within MOM's work-pass exemption.

MOM foreign student work exemption ↗
HEALTH + INSURANCE

University health fees and insurance are institution-specific.

Major universities usually include mandatory student health or insurance charges, but these do not replace the need to understand what is and is not covered.

University charges

Miscellaneous health fees may apply

NUS and other institutions levy student services and health-related fees in addition to tuition.

Before travel

Check medical requirements

Follow the ICA IPA letter and university enrolment instructions for any required medical examination or insurance evidence.

AFTER GRADUATION

Eligible graduates can stay to seek work, but actual employment requires a work pass.

ICA has a Long-Term Visit Pass category for graduates from recognised Institutes of Higher Learning who are seeking employment in Singapore.

Job search

ICA LTVP route

Eligible graduates from listed institutions can apply online for an LTVP to remain in Singapore while seeking employment.

Employment Pass

Current minimum starts at S$5,600

For new EP applications before 1 January 2027, the minimum qualifying salary starts at S$5,600 outside financial services and rises with age. It increases to S$6,000 for new applications from 1 January 2027.

S Pass

Current minimum starts at S$3,300

The current qualifying salary starts at S$3,300 outside financial services and increases to S$3,600 for new applications from 1 January 2027.

Singapore should not be marketed as having an automatic open graduate work visa. A graduate who finds employment needs an employer-sponsored or otherwise qualifying MOM work pass.
ICA graduate job-search LTVP ↗
WHERE TO LIVE

In Singapore, commute and housing type matter more than city choice.

Students usually choose accommodation based on campus access, MRT connections, budget and whether university housing is available.

Kent Ridge / Clementi

NUS access

Useful for NUS students, with campus and western Singapore connections.

Jurong / Boon Lay

NTU access

Closer to NTU and generally more practical than central-city housing for daily commute.

City centre

SMU + business access

Very convenient for SMU and internships, but housing costs can be substantially higher.

East / North

Trade cost for commute

Some students choose lower-cost rooms farther from campus and rely on MRT or bus connections.

AFTER ARRIVAL

Complete ICA formalities before treating the Student's Pass as active.

Your university and ICA will guide the final enrolment, biometrics and pass issuance steps.

01

Complete university registration

Activate enrolment and submit originals where required.

02

Attend Student's Pass formalities

Complete biometrics at ICA or an institution-arranged offsite enrolment.

03

Receive digital pass

Follow ICA instructions for issuance and digital access.

04

Set up bank + local services

A local account is useful for rent, scholarship payments and employment.

05

Track pass expiry

Student work rights end when the Student's Pass expires unless another valid work or immigration status is obtained.

Admission, immigration, student work, tuition and graduate employment are controlled by different agencies.

MAKE IT PERSONAL

Choose Singapore with admission strength, real tuition, housing and graduate employment together.

Singapore can be a high-value destination for strong Bangladeshi students, but the decision must account for competitive admission, high living costs, Tuition Grant conditions and the work pass required after graduation.
