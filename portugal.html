PORTUGAL · BANGLADESH STUDENT GUIDE

Study in Portugal
from Bangladesh.

Portugal offers respected public universities, growing English-taught study, lower tuition than many Western European destinations, legal work alongside higher education, and a strong graduate transition route for Master and PhD students. For Bangladeshi applicants, admission, national study visa and AIMA residence should be planned as one connected process.

Portugal in one view

Strong fit for students who want an EU degree, work access during study, comparatively moderate tuition and a realistic transition into employment after graduation.

!

Do not use the Portuguese domestic €697 public tuition cap as your international-student fee

The Portuguese public tuition cap applies to domestic or equivalent students in specified cycles. Universities set separate international-student tuition. Your actual international fee must come from the programme page or offer letter.

ADMISSION

Portugal has a dedicated international-student admission route for non-EU Bachelor applicants.

DGES allows non-EU applicants holding a qualification that gives access to higher education in the country where it was awarded to apply under the international-student competition. Universities then apply their own subject, grade and examination rules.

Bachelor

HSC or equivalent higher-education entry qualification

Bangladeshi applicants normally use SSC and HSC records plus any subject or entrance evidence required by the chosen institution.

Master

Recognised Bachelor degree

Programme relevance, GPA, portfolio, interview or professional experience can matter depending on the course.

PhD

Graduate qualification + research fit

Doctoral selection commonly considers research proposal, academic record and supervisor or faculty fit.

Portugal does not use one national HSC percentage or CGPA cut-off for every international student. Admission standards are institution and programme specific.

HIGHER EDUCATION SYSTEM

Portugal has both university and polytechnic higher education.

The university sector is more academically and research oriented, while polytechnics often emphasise applied and professionally focused education.

Public universitiesResearch + broad disciplinesLisbon, Porto, Coimbra, Minho, Aveiro and other major institutions.
PolytechnicsApplied + professionalStrong options in business, engineering, tourism, design and technology.
Private universitiesProfessional + internationalInstitution-specific tuition and English-medium choices.
Research schoolsMaster + PhDStrong graduate research ecosystems in science, engineering, economics and health.

INTAKES

September is the main intake, with multiple application phases at many universities.

Portuguese institutions often open international applications in several rounds from late winter or spring through summer. Some Master and doctoral programmes use different calendars.

MAIN INTAKE

September

Broadest choice across Bachelor, Master and PhD programmes.

OTHER STARTS

February, selected programmes

Some postgraduate and institution-specific programmes offer second-semester entry.

Apply early enough to leave time for admission confirmation, accommodation, visa processing and AIMA residence preparation. Later university phases may exist only if places remain.
PORTUGUESE · ENGLISH

English is strongest at Master and PhD level. Portuguese opens the widest Bachelor choice.

Language evidence is set by the university. English-medium programmes commonly accept IELTS, TOEFL or equivalent proof, while Portuguese-taught programmes can require Portuguese proficiency.

English BachelorGrowing but limitedMore common in business, technology and international programmes.
English MasterWidely availableEspecially business, economics, science and engineering.
Portuguese studyBroader choiceUseful for both university access and employment integration.
Exact IELTSUniversity decidesNo single national score applies to all programmes.
LIVING COST

Housing is the main budget variable.

Lisbon is usually the most expensive student market. Porto can be more manageable, while Coimbra, Braga and Aveiro can reduce housing pressure further.

Lisbon private room example~€300–400+ / month

These are university guidance examples, not a national guaranteed budget. Current market rents can be higher, especially in central Lisbon and Porto.

TUITION

International tuition is set by the institution, not by Portugal’s domestic public-fee cap.

International fees can still be competitive by Western European standards, but they vary widely by school and programme.

ULisboa Sciences example

€3,500 / year

For 2026 to 27, Ciências ULisboa lists €3,500 annual tuition for international Bachelor and many Master students.

ISEG Bachelor example

€7,500 / year

ISEG lists a provisional €7,500 international undergraduate tuition fee for 2026 to 27.

ISEG Master example

€4,100–6,000 / year

2026 to 27 non-EU Master tuition varies by programme type.

These are real 2026 university examples, not a national range. Always replace them with the exact programme fee from your university.

SCHOLARSHIPS

Portugal does not have one universal scholarship for every non-EU degree student.

Funding is usually university-specific, research-based or connected to European mobility and doctoral programmes.

University awards

Merit and faculty funding

Institutions may offer merit awards, tuition reductions or programme-specific scholarships.

Erasmus+

Mobility funding

Relevant to eligible exchange, joint and mobility programmes rather than every full-degree international student.

Doctoral research

FCT + project funding

PhD and research applicants should investigate Fundação para a Ciência e a Tecnologia calls and funded research projects.

Do not treat Portugal’s domestic social scholarship system as automatically available to a newly arriving international student. Check the eligibility rules of each funding scheme.
STUDY RESIDENCE VISA · BANGLADESH

The long-term study route is a Portuguese national residence visa followed by AIMA residence.

Bangladeshi applicants can use Portugal’s official VFS Bangladesh service for visa submission information. Higher-education study should be filed under the appropriate national study-residence category rather than a short-stay tourist visa.

01

Receive university admission

Obtain admission or proof that you meet the higher-education admission conditions.

02

Prepare visa documents

Passport, photos, criminal record, insurance, accommodation and means of subsistence form the core file.

03

Use Portugal VFS Bangladesh

Check the current national-visa category, appointment and submission instructions for Bangladesh.

04

Submit residence visa

The study residence visa is the entry route for long-term higher education.

05

Travel to Portugal

Carry admission, accommodation and financial evidence with you.

06

Complete AIMA residence

Higher-education students obtain the Article 91 residence permit after meeting the residence requirements.

07

Residence validity

The current AIMA rule allows up to three years, renewable, or the programme duration when shorter.

08

Maintain study purpose

Keep enrolment, resources, accommodation and legal status current.

The general Portuguese government service currently lists a €90 residence-visa fee for higher-education mobility cases, but Bangladesh applicants should use the exact fee and category shown by VFS and the competent Portuguese mission at submission time.

SPOUSE · CHILDREN

Family reunification is possible, but it is a separate residence process.

Portugal has family reunification routes for qualifying residence holders. Relationship evidence, accommodation, resources and the principal student’s residence status all matter.

Planning

Confirm family eligibility before departure

Do not assume the spouse and children simply receive the same student status as the principal applicant.

Resources

Household funding rises

A larger family changes accommodation and means-of-subsistence planning and should be calculated before accepting the offer.

AIMA family and residence services ↗
WORK WHILE STUDYING

Higher-education residence holders can legally work alongside their studies.

AIMA states that holders of a higher-education study residence permit may carry out employed or self-employed professional activity complementary to the activity that created the residence right.

Employment

Allowed

Student residence can support paid employment while you continue to fulfil the study purpose.

Self-employment

Also possible

AIMA explicitly recognises both subordinated and independent professional activity.

Hours

No simple national 20-hour rule shown by AIMA

Do not copy another country’s weekly cap. Your work must remain complementary to the studies and comply with Portuguese labour, tax and social-security rules.

AIMA student work rights ↗
HEALTH + INSURANCE

Insurance begins before residence.

The residence-visa file requires travel insurance covering necessary medical costs, urgent treatment and repatriation. After arrival, healthcare access depends on your residence and registration status.

Visa stage

Travel medical insurance

Valid coverage is part of the national study-residence visa file.

Portugal

Complete health registration

Follow university and Portuguese public-health guidance after your residence status is established.

AFTER GRADUATION

Portugal treats Bachelor graduates differently from Master and PhD graduates.

The graduate route is useful, but it should not be simplified into one universal post-study visa for every level.

Bachelor graduate

Can move into work residence with qualifying activity

AIMA has a specific Article 122 route for graduates of the 1st cycle who have completed studies and want to carry out employed or independent professional activity.

Master / PhD graduate

Up to 1 year to seek work or create a business

Graduates of the 2nd and 3rd cycles can use a maximum one-year period to look for work or establish a company compatible with their qualifications.

The one-year search period is specifically stated by AIMA for Master and PhD graduates. Bachelor graduates should not be promised the same open search period automatically.

WHERE TO STUDY

Portugal offers different cost and career profiles by city.

The city can materially change housing cost, programme choice and employer access.

Lisbon

Largest university + employer market

Finance, technology, business, research and international companies, with the highest housing pressure.

Porto

Engineering + business + strong student life

Major academic and employer market with somewhat lower living costs than Lisbon in many cases.

Coimbra

Historic student city

Strong university identity and potentially lower housing costs.

Braga / Guimarães

Technology + lower-cost north

University of Minho ecosystem with engineering and innovation links.

Aveiro

Technology + compact city

Strong engineering and science with a smaller-city environment.

Faro

Tourism + southern Portugal

Hospitality, tourism, marine and regional study options in the Algarve.

AFTER ARRIVAL

The residence visa gets you in. AIMA residence keeps you in legal long-term study status.

Use the university international office and current AIMA procedure to complete the first-month formalities.

01

Complete university enrolment

Confirm your place and obtain current enrolment evidence.

02

Set up accommodation

Keep valid proof of your Portuguese address.

03

Tax + social setup where needed

NIF and NISS become important when you work or interact with Portuguese services.

04

Complete AIMA residence

Attend the required residence process and provide the current study, funds and accommodation documents.

05

Keep residence conditions current

Maintain enrolment and renew or transition your status when required.
