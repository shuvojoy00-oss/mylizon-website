BANGLADESH ELIGIBILITY

The U.S. does not use one national HSC or CGPA cut-off.

Every institution sets its own admission requirements. U.S. admissions can also consider English, essays, recommendations, activities, standardized tests and overall fit — not only GPA.

HSC

Bangladesh HSC can support freshman admission.

Many U.S. universities evaluate HSC/secondary credentials directly, but required GPA, subjects and testing vary by institution.

Community College

2+2 can be a lower-cost Bachelor route.

Students can complete an associate degree at community college, then transfer to a four-year university if credits and articulation rules line up.

Masters

Graduate admission is department-specific.

Universities may assess GPA, prerequisites, GRE/GMAT where required, statement of purpose, references, research and work experience.

Low CGPA

There is no one U.S. answer.

Professional experience, strong recent academics, a suitable university tier, bridge study or a strong overall application can matter.

Study Gap

Explain the timeline clearly.

Work, business, research, family responsibilities and why the chosen program makes sense now should form one coherent story.

Research

Assistantships can change affordability.

Research-based Master’s and PhD applicants should look for funded positions, teaching/research assistantships and faculty fit, not just tuition discounts.

SEVP-CERTIFIED SCHOOLS

First verify the school can issue an I-20.

F-1 students must attend an SEVP-certified school. Use the official Study in the States School Search rather than assuming every U.S. college can sponsor an F-1 student.

Universities

Public and private universities offer Bachelor, Master’s and doctoral study. Cost, selectivity, research strength and aid differ dramatically.

Community colleges

Two-year associate programs can lower tuition and create a 2+2 transfer route, but transfer planning must be done carefully.

English-language programs

Some SEVP-certified schools can issue I-20s for intensive English programs. Work and progression rules differ from degree study.

IELTS · TOEFL · PTE · TESTING

The school sets admission English and testing rules.

The F-1 visa does not use one national IELTS score. Universities decide which English tests and standardized tests they accept.

English

TOEFL and IELTS are widely used; many institutions also accept PTE or Duolingo. Minimums vary by program.

SAT / ACT

Many U.S. universities remain test-optional or use institution-specific policies. Scholarship consideration can have different testing rules.

GRE / GMAT

Graduate programs decide whether tests are required, optional or waived. Never assume one policy applies across the university.

MONEY & I-20 FUNDING

The USA has no single national “maintenance amount.”

Each school calculates its own estimated annual cost of attendance for the I-20. Before issuing the I-20, schools review proof that the student can cover tuition and living costs.

Your I-20 typically shows

Tuition & feesSchool-specific
Living expensesSchool/location-specific
Other costsBooks / insurance / dependants etc.
Student fundingPersonal + sponsor + school funds

What the visa officer can ask

Department of State guidance says the consular officer may ask how you will pay all educational, living and travel costs. Your funding should be understandable, credible and accessible.

BANGLADESH FINANCIAL EVIDENCE

Show the source, relationship and sustainability of the money.

Bank statements / deposits

Show available funds and history. Large recent deposits should have a credible documented source.

Sponsor evidence

Relationship, sponsor income, employment/business, tax records and ability to fund the student can support the story.

Scholarship / assistantship

Official university funding can appear on the I-20 and materially reduce the amount the family needs to demonstrate.

Education loan

Approved education loans can be part of the funding plan when consistent with school and visa documentation.

SCHOLARSHIPS & ASSISTANTSHIPS

U.S. funding can be excellent — but it is competitive and institution-specific.

Merit scholarships

Many universities offer international merit awards based on academics, testing, talent or holistic admission.

Need-based aid

A smaller group of institutions can provide substantial need-based aid to international undergraduates. Admission can be highly competitive.

Graduate assistantships

Teaching and research assistantships may include stipends, tuition remission or both. Research fit and department funding are critical.

Community college

Scholarships exist but are generally less common than at four-year institutions. The main cost advantage is lower tuition and transfer strategy.

F-1 STUDENT VISA

From admission to the visa interview.

01

Apply to an SEVP-certified school

Get admitted to a school authorized to enroll F-1 students.

02

Financial review & I-20

After admission and proof of funding, the school creates your SEVIS record and issues Form I-20.

03

Pay SEVIS I-901 fee

The current F/M student SEVIS I-901 fee is USD 350. Keep the confirmation for the interview.

04

Complete DS-160

Use your SEVIS ID and school information from the I-20 when completing the online visa application.

05

Pay visa fee & schedule interview

The current F-1 nonimmigrant visa application fee is USD 185.

06

Prepare for interview

Bring required documents and be ready to explain academics, finances, why this school/program and your intent to depart after study.

07

Interview & fingerprints

A consular officer decides whether you qualify. Administrative processing may be required in some cases.

08

Visa & travel

New students can receive an F visa up to 365 days before the program starts, but cannot enter more than 30 days before the start date.

VISA COSTS

Separate the school process from U.S. government fees.

F-1 visa application

USD 185 nonimmigrant visa application processing fee.

SEVIS I-901

USD 350 for F/M students. F-2 dependants do not pay a separate SEVIS I-901 fee.

School costs

Application fees, deposits, credential evaluation, courier, testing and health-insurance costs are institution-specific.

DOCUMENTS · BANGLADESH

U.S. admission documents and F-1 interview documents serve different purposes.

University / college admission

Passport, SSC/HSC or previous degree transcripts/certificates, English test, SAT/ACT/GRE/GMAT where required, essays/SOP, recommendations, CV, portfolio or research materials depending on program.

I-20 / funding documents

Bank statements, sponsor evidence, scholarship/assistantship letters, education loan or other proof meeting the school’s I-20 funding requirement.

F-1 visa interview

Passport, DS-160 confirmation, visa-fee receipt where applicable, photo if needed, signed Form I-20, SEVIS fee confirmation and any additional academic/financial documents requested.

Employment / study-gap evidence

Job certificate, experience letters, salary records, business documents or other evidence can help explain the student’s timeline and why the new study is logical.

Police clearance

A police clearance certificate is not listed as a standard required F-1 interview document for every applicant. Do not confuse immigrant-visa police-certificate rules with the standard F-1 process.

F-2 SPOUSE & CHILDREN

Family can accompany an F-1 student — but F-2 work rights are very limited.

Separate I-20 for each dependant

Spouse and unmarried children under 21 need their own F-2 I-20 and visa application.

No SEVIS fee for F-2

The school creates the dependant records, but F-2 dependants do not pay a separate I-901 SEVIS fee.

F-2 spouse cannot work

Current USCIS guidance says F-2 status does not provide employment authorization.

Study restrictions matter

F-2 children can attend K-12 school. An F-2 spouse who wants full academic study generally needs to change to F-1 status.

WORK & PRACTICAL TRAINING

On-campus work, CPT and OPT are three different permissions.

On campus

Usually up to 20 hours/week while school is in session.

F-1 students may have limited on-campus employment under the F-1 rules and school authorization framework.

CPT

Curricular Practical Training

Internship, cooperative education or required/established curriculum training before program completion. DSO authorization is central.

OPT

Optional Practical Training

Up to 12 months per higher education level, directly related to the student’s major. USCIS issues the EAD.

STEM OPT

Additional 24 months

Eligible STEM graduates can apply for a 24-month extension with an E-Verify employer and Form I-983 training plan.

CPT DETAILS

CPT is not “free work permission.” It must be curricular.

During the program

CPT is used before the program end date for qualifying practical training that is part of the curriculum or established cooperative arrangement.

Full-time CPT warning

One year or more of full-time CPT can eliminate eligibility for post-completion OPT. Part-time CPT does not have the same effect.

H-1B & LONG-TERM EMPLOYMENT

Study does not automatically lead to a Green Card.

H-1B

A U.S. employer can sponsor eligible specialty-occupation work. Cap-subject H-1B has a 65,000 regular cap plus a 20,000 U.S. advanced-degree exemption, with separate cap-exempt employers.

Cap-gap

Some F-1 students with timely qualifying H-1B filings can receive cap-gap extensions of status/employment under applicable rules.

Employment-based Green Card

Permanent residence is a separate employer/self-petition immigration process. Degree choice does not guarantee sponsorship or a Green Card.

COST & LOCATION

New York and California should not be budgeted like the Midwest or South.

Northeast

New York, Boston and nearby areas offer dense university and employer networks, but living costs can be among the highest.

California / West Coast

Major technology and research ecosystems, but housing costs can be very high in large metros.

Midwest

Many strong universities and community colleges with generally lower housing costs than major coastal cities.

South

Large public universities and growing metro areas often combine broad program choice with lower living costs.

EducationUSA specifically recommends considering South, Midwest and more rural locations when controlling living costs.
ARRIVAL

Your visa gets you to the border. CBP grants admission.

Before departure

Passport, F-1 visa, signed I-20, SEVIS receipt, school contact, funding evidence, housing, health insurance and travel plan.

Entry timing

New F-1 students cannot enter more than 30 days before the program start date on Form I-20.

At the border

CBP decides admission and your I-94 status. Review your admission record and contact the school if anything looks wrong.

First week

Check in with the international office/DSO, complete orientation, register full-time, understand work restrictions, bank/phone/transport and health-insurance arrangements.
