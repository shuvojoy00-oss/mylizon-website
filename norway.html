BANGLADESH ELIGIBILITY

Norway’s GSU list gives Bangladesh a clear national baseline.

The Higher Education Entrance Qualification is binding for first-cycle admission. Individual universities can still add programme-specific subject and grade requirements.

HSC

HSC + 1 year university study.

Bangladesh Higher Secondary Certificate normally needs at least one completed year of recognised university education for general Norwegian higher-education entrance eligibility.

One-year requirement

The extra study must be recognised higher education.

The additional year should be successfully completed at a state-recognised institution. NHH currently describes this as at least 60 ECTS-equivalent full-time study for Bangladesh applicants.

English

Bangladesh secondary education does not automatically cover the English requirement.

The GSU list marks Bangladesh English as not covered, so accepted test/course evidence is normally needed unless another exemption applies.

Norwegian

Norwegian is required for ordinary Norwegian-taught Bachelor admission.

Applicants from outside the Nordic region must document Norwegian under GSU rules unless applying to an English-taught programme with a different institutional route.

Masters

Master admission is university-specific.

A recognised Bachelor degree, sufficient academic depth in the relevant subject, grades and English requirements are assessed by the university.

Recognition

HK-dir recognition is separate from admission.

You normally apply directly to the university for admission. General recognition of foreign higher education can be useful for work or documentation but is not usually required just to submit a Master’s application.

NORWEGIAN HIGHER EDUCATION

Universities, specialised institutions and university colleges all matter.

Universities

Research institutions offering Bachelor, Master and PhD study, including Oslo, Bergen, NTNU, Tromsø, Stavanger and NMBU.

Specialised universities

Institutions such as NHH focus on particular areas like economics and business.

University colleges

Applied/professional institutions offer programmes in technology, health, business, education and other fields.

Accreditation matters

For a university study permit, the place of study must be an approved/accredited institution recognised under Norwegian quality-assurance rules.

IELTS · PTE · ENGLISH

Norway has a national GSU English baseline, but programmes can demand more.

GSU examples

HK-dir lists IELTS Academic 5.0, TOEFL iBT 60 and PTE Academic 51 among ways to document the general GSU English requirement.

University programmes

English-taught Bachelor and Master programmes often set higher admission scores than the GSU baseline. Always follow the programme page.

MONEY & MAINTENANCE

The 2026/27 UDI maintenance requirement is NOK 170,368 for the academic year.

Tuition is separate.

If tuition applies, you must also document that it is paid or that you have enough money to cover it. Funds can come from loans, grants, own funds in a Norwegian account or the institution’s deposit account, and qualifying part-time-job income can sometimes be included.

TUITION

Most degree-seeking non-EU/EEA students now pay tuition.

Public institutions

Degree-seeking students from outside the EU/EEA and Switzerland must normally pay tuition fees. The amount varies by institution and programme.

Common exemptions

Doctoral candidates, institutional exchange students and students under certain schemes such as NORPART/NORHED can be exempt.

Semester fee

Study in Norway currently estimates an additional student welfare semester fee of about NOK 1,000 per semester.

LIVING COST & HOUSING

Norway is expensive, and Oslo should not be budgeted like a smaller city.

Student housing

Student welfare organisations manage many student residences and can be cheaper than private-market housing.

Private rent

Oslo and central Bergen/Trondheim can be substantially more expensive than smaller cities. Deposit requirements also matter.

Real budget

The UDI maintenance figure is an immigration minimum, not a promise that every student can live comfortably on exactly that amount.

SCHOLARSHIPS

Norway has fewer broad full-degree scholarships than many students expect.

Institutional scholarships

Scholarships and tuition waivers are institution/programme-specific and are much less standardised than in some other destinations.

Exchange and cooperation schemes

NORPART, NORHED, Erasmus Mundus and other cooperation routes can include tuition exemptions or funding for eligible participants.

PhD positions

Many Norwegian PhD positions are salaried employment and doctoral candidates are normally exempt from public-university tuition.

UDI STUDY PERMIT

Admission, finance, tuition and housing all matter in the permit decision.

01

Admission

Obtain full-time admission to an accredited university or university college.

02

Tuition

Pay required tuition or document an exemption / enough money to cover any outstanding tuition.

03

Transfer maintenance funds

Document NOK 170,368 for the 2026/27 academic year through acceptable UDI channels.

04

Housing

You must have somewhere to live in Norway.

05

Apply online

Register the study-permit application with UDI and pay the current application fee.

06

Submit documents / biometrics

Follow the Norway/VFS instructions applicable to Bangladesh for passport, document submission and biometrics.

07

UDI decision

UDI assesses admission, finances, housing, tuition and whether the circumstances indicate that you are likely to return after study.

08

Travel & residence card

After approval, follow entry and police/residence-card instructions and complete university registration after arrival.

PERMIT COSTS

UDI study and work permits have separate fees.

Study permit · adult

NOK 5,400 for applicants over 18, including renewals under the current fee table.

Study permit · under 18

NOK 2,700 under the current UDI fee table.

Skilled-worker permit

NOK 6,300 for adult work-residence applications under the current fee table.

DOCUMENTS · BANGLADESH

Separate admission evidence from UDI permit evidence.

Bachelor admission

HSC plus evidence of the required additional recognised university study, official transcripts, English/Norwegian evidence where applicable and programme-specific subject documents.

Master’s admission

Bachelor degree certificate, transcripts, English evidence and programme-specific academic prerequisites, CV, motivation, references or portfolio/research documents where required.

Study permit

Passport, university admission, proof of full-time study, maintenance funds, tuition payment/funding, housing documentation and UDI application documents.

Funds

UDI accepts specified combinations of loans, grants and own funds. Students without a Norwegian bank account can often use the educational institution’s deposit account according to the university’s process.

Return / study intention

UDI requires the circumstances in the home country to indicate that return after studies is likely. Keep your programme choice, career plan and Bangladesh ties coherent.

SPOUSE & FAMILY

University students can qualify for family immigration, but there are additional conditions.

Eligible student status

The reference person must have or have applied for a student residence permit at a university/university college and generally have at least one year of studies left.

Income requirement

UDI applies a separate family-immigration income requirement. The amount can be met through qualifying employment, study loans/grants and funds held by the student or applicant.

Spouse / cohabitant

Relationship, age, identity and cohabitation/marriage requirements apply. The family application is separate from the student permit.

WORK WHILE STUDYING

Eligible students can work up to 20 hours per week during study.

20 hours / week

A university/university-college study permit normally includes permission for up to 20 hours of part-time work per week, including remote work.

Full-time holidays

You can work full-time during recognised holidays.

No self-employment

A student permit does not allow you to be self-employed or run your own business.

Renewal can reassess work rights

When renewing, UDI can consider whether the institution believes you can continue combining study and part-time work.

HEALTHCARE & IDENTIFICATION

Your length of stay affects registration and healthcare access.

Norwegian ID / D-number

Students may receive a national identity number or D-number depending on residence length and registration circumstances.

Healthcare

Longer-term residents can become members of Norway’s National Insurance Scheme under applicable rules. Universities also provide student welfare and health-service information.

SKILLED WORKER & PERMANENT STAY

The real long-term route begins when you move onto a qualifying work permit.

Skilled-worker residence

You normally need a qualifying job offer matching skilled-worker requirements and employment conditions that meet Norwegian standards.

Student time does not count

Time on a study permit does not form the basis for permanent residence. The graduate job-seeker permit also does not count.

Work permits can count

Qualifying skilled-worker residence can form part of the pathway toward permanent residence if all relevant residence and integration conditions are met.

CITIES

Cost, climate and job market vary sharply across Norway.

Oslo

Largest employer market and broad university choice, with the highest housing pressure.

Trondheim

Technology and engineering hub centred around NTNU.

Bergen

Major university city with marine, energy, business and climate-research strengths.

Stavanger

Energy, engineering and business ecosystem.

Tromsø

Arctic university city with health, fisheries, climate and northern-region research.

Ås

Smaller university environment around NMBU, close to the wider Oslo region.

ARRIVAL

UDI approval is only the beginning of your Norwegian setup.

Before departure

Passport, permit decision/entry visa where required, admission, financial and tuition evidence, housing and university arrival instructions.

After arrival

Follow police/residence-card instructions and complete university enrolment.

Practical setup

Arrange identification number/D-number where applicable, bank account, tax card if working, phone, transport and student welfare membership.

Work

Stay within the 20-hour weekly limit during study periods and check that your permit continues to include part-time work permission.
