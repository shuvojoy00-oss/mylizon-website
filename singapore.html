SINGAPORE · BANGLADESH STUDENT GUIDE

Study in Singapore
from Bangladesh.

Singapore offers world-class universities, English-medium education, strong technology and business ecosystems, structured student work rights and direct access to one of Asia’s strongest employer markets. The trade-off is cost: tuition and living expenses can be high, and there is no universal open post-study work visa.

ADMISSION

Singapore admissions are highly university and programme specific.

Top public universities are competitive. International applicants are assessed on academic results, subject prerequisites, English proficiency and, for some programmes, tests, portfolios or interviews.

Bachelor

Strong HSC profile + subject fit

Bangladeshi students should expect competitive academic requirements at NUS, NTU and SMU, especially for computing, engineering, business and health programmes.

Master

Relevant Bachelor degree

GPA, academic fit, work experience, GMAT/GRE or portfolio may matter depending on programme.

PhD

Research profile + supervisor fit

Research proposal, prior academic performance and funding availability are central.

UNIVERSITIES

Singapore’s higher education market is small but exceptionally concentrated.

Most international students compare a handful of highly recognised autonomous universities and selected specialist institutions.

National University of Singapore

INTAKES

August is the main undergraduate intake.

Public universities generally admit undergraduates once per academic year, while graduate programmes can have August, January or programme-specific starts.

MAIN UNDERGRADUATE

August

Primary intake for NUS, NTU, SMU and most autonomous universities.

GRADUATE

August / January / programme-specific

Master and PhD calendars vary considerably.

Singapore’s top universities often close international applications many months before August. Plan admission before planning the Student's Pass.
ENGLISH

English is the main language of university instruction.

English requirements depend on your prior curriculum and institution. Universities may accept IELTS, TOEFL or other evidence, and some applicants can be exempt based on previous English-medium education.

LIVING COST

Singapore is expensive, especially for housing.

NUS’s 2026 international prospectus estimates about S$6,000 in annual living expenses plus about S$5,750 in accommodation for a 35-week study period, giving a conservative on-campus planning figure around S$11,750.

NUS 2026 planning example

Living expenses~S$6,000 / academic year
Accommodation~S$5,750 / 35 weeks
Combined conservative example~S$11,750
Private housingCan be materially higher
Transport + foodBudget separately if off campus
This is an NUS planning estimate, not a national immigration minimum. Private-market rent can push total costs much higher.

TUITION + MOE TUITION GRANT

Singapore tuition has two very different price paths: subsidised and non-subsidised.

International undergraduates may compete for the MOE Tuition Grant. If awarded and accepted, fees fall substantially, but the student must work for a Singapore entity for three years after graduation.

NTU 2026 subsidised example

~S$21,400 / year

For many non-lab programmes for non-ASEAN international students receiving the Tuition Grant.

NTU 2026 non-subsidised example

~S$36,350–40,600 / year

Current standard programme examples without the Tuition Grant.

Medicine example

~S$91,250 / year subsidised

Medicine is dramatically more expensive and follows its own fee structure.

The MOE Tuition Grant is valuable, but it creates a real post-graduation service obligation. Students should treat the lower fee and the three-year work commitment as one decision.

SCHOLARSHIPS + FINANCIAL AID

Top universities offer competitive scholarships, but international awards are selective.

NUS, NTU, SMU and other institutions offer merit scholarships and financial-aid schemes. International-student eligibility can depend on whether the applicant accepts the MOE Tuition Grant.

NUS / NTU scholarships

Merit-based

Highly competitive awards can cover tuition and sometimes living support.

MOE Tuition Grant

Fee subsidy, not a scholarship

International recipients must fulfil the three-year Singapore work obligation after graduation.

Financial aid

Eligibility varies

NUS currently links international undergraduate financial-aid eligibility to acceptance of the MOE Tuition Grant.

STUDENT'S PASS · ICA

Full-time international students need a Student's Pass unless they already hold another qualifying Singapore pass.

ICA manages the Student's Pass. The application process depends on the type of institution, but the core requirement is admission to an approved full-time course.

01

Secure admission

Receive an offer from an approved institution.

02

Institution registers the study place

For Institutes of Higher Learning, the school provides the information needed for the ICA application.

03

Submit Student's Pass application

Complete the ICA e-service with passport, personal, education and financial information as required.

04

Receive in-principle approval

Follow ICA’s entry instructions and any visa requirement attached to the approval.

05

Travel to Singapore

Carry the admission and ICA documents needed for entry.

06

Complete formalities

Finish any required medical, identity or enrolment checks.

07

Receive digital Student's Pass

ICA issues the Student's Pass digitally through FileSG / Singpass after completion.

Only approved full-time study qualifies. Evening, weekend and general part-time courses do not qualify for a Student's Pass.

FAMILY

Singapore is not a simple dependant destination for ordinary international students.

A Student's Pass does not automatically allow the student to sponsor a spouse or children for dependant residence. Family options depend on separate immigration eligibility and should be checked before enrolment.

Spouse / children

No automatic student dependant route

Do not assume family accompaniment works like Australia, Canada or Finland.

Parent / grandparent

Limited route for younger students

ICA has a Long-Term Visit Pass category for one parent or grandparent of a child studying in Singapore, subject to sponsorship rules.

WORK WHILE STUDYING

Eligible Student's Pass holders can work up to 16 hours per week during term.

The Ministry of Manpower work-pass exemption applies only if you study full-time at an institution on MOM’s approved list.

During term

Maximum 16 hours / week

Or participation in a qualifying industrial attachment or internship contributing toward graduation.

During vacation

Work without a work pass

Eligible full-time students at approved institutions may work during vacation without a separate work pass.

Exchange students

Not allowed under this exemption

MOM specifically excludes exchange students from the ordinary foreign-student work exemption.

AFTER GRADUATION

Eligible graduates can stay to seek work, but employment requires a separate work pass.

Graduates from recognised Singapore Institutes of Higher Learning can apply for an ICA Long-Term Visit Pass for employment search.

Graduate LTVP

Job-search stay

ICA accepts applications from graduates of eligible Institutes of Higher Learning seeking employment.

Once hired

Employer applies for work pass

The LTVP itself is not a general work pass. Employment normally requires an Employment Pass, S Pass or another appropriate MOM status.

ICA currently charges S$45 to submit the graduate LTVP application and S$60 for issuance, plus S$30 for a Multiple Journey Visa if applicable. Current stated processing time is within six weeks for complete applications.

WHERE TO LIVE

Singapore is one city-state, so the real choice is campus and housing location.

Commute time and housing type matter more than choosing a separate city.

Kent Ridge

NUS area

Convenient for NUS students, with campus housing and western Singapore access.

Jurong West

NTU area

Closer to NTU and generally farther from the central business district.

Bras Basah / City

SMU area

Excellent central access but usually higher private housing costs.

Punggol

SIT area

New campus ecosystem with transport links to northeastern Singapore.

AFTER ARRIVAL

Complete ICA and university formalities quickly.

Singapore’s digital systems make the process efficient, but your Student's Pass and enrolment must be finalised correctly.

01

University enrolment

Complete registration and orientation.

02

Complete ICA formalities

Attend any required appointment or identity checks.

03

Retrieve digital Student's Pass

Use FileSG or Singpass once ICA issues it.

04

Banking + mobile + housing

Set up day-to-day essentials and keep your local address current.

05

Work only if eligible

Confirm that your institution is on the MOM-approved list before taking employment.
