IELTS Reading Matching Headings: How to Find the Main Idea

What does the question ask you to do?

IELTS provides a set of headings and asks you to match each with the paragraph or section it best summarizes. There are more headings than needed. It tests whether you can identify the main idea rather than a supporting detail. Matching Headings is used in both Academic and General Training Reading.

A practical step-by-step method

  1. Survey the headings. Notice the difference between similar options. Underline the words showing the topic or purpose.
  2. Skim a paragraph for its main idea. Check the opening and closing sentences for clues, but read further when they do not represent the whole paragraph.
  3. Summarize the paragraph in your own words. Ask: is it explaining a cause, benefit, challenge, change or solution?
  4. Match meaning, not identical words. A heading may use synonyms or a broader summary of the paragraph.
  5. Eliminate used choices and revisit difficult paragraphs. Don't spend all your available time on one uncertain heading.

Important: The first or last sentence is a useful clue, not a fixed rule. No sentence position or shortcut guarantees a given percentage of correct answers.

Try an original practice example

Paragraph A

Many city libraries now lend more than books. Visitors can borrow tools, attend free digital-skills workshops and use shared workspaces. These services have helped libraries respond to changing community needs while preserving their original role as places for learning.

Choose the best heading:

  1. The decline of traditional reading habits
  2. How libraries are expanding their services
  3. The high cost of building new libraries
Show the answer and explanation

ii — How libraries are expanding their services. The paragraph lists several new services and links them to community needs. It does not chiefly discuss declining reading habits or construction costs.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing a heading just because the same noun appears in both places.
  • Matching one interesting example instead of the paragraph's overall message.
  • Reading every line slowly before establishing the paragraph's purpose.
  • Assuming the opening sentence always gives the full answer.

Matching Headings vs Matching Information

Matching Headings asks for the overall main idea of a paragraph. Matching Information asks where a particular detail, example, explanation or claim appears. These tasks require different reading approaches.

Frequently asked questions

Should I read the full passage first?

Not necessarily. Start with the task and skim for each paragraph's main idea. Read more deeply when the summary remains unclear.

Are headings in the same order as the paragraphs?

No. You must match each relevant paragraph with the appropriate heading; don't assume list order will match paragraph order.

Is matching one keyword enough?

No. Choose the heading that summarizes the paragraph as a whole, even if the wording is different.
